Let’s just say it. Portfolios are the absolute worst. 

Not because UX work is bad, but because the pressure to build a perfect portfolio is so high that most of us would rather wrestle a bear than finalize and publish our first case study. We mythologize them. We overthink them. We believe our entire career is riding on how elegant our screenshots look next to a ubiquitous “iterative design process” graphic.

If I’m being honest, I’ve spent too much time belaboring over my portfolio(s). But this time around, I’m taking a different approach.

The first time someone told me to put my UX work on GitHub, I blinked in confusion. "GitHub? For UX?" She was a recruiter. A smart one. But the idea sounded like suggesting a pastry chef host their portfolio on Stack Overflow. Still, the idea stuck with me. I kept circling back to it. Because I’ve never liked the traditional UX portfolio. Neither as a creator, nor as a reader.

But the more I thought about it, the more it made sense. GitHub isn’t just for devs. It’s a place to store, track, and update your thinking. That is exactly what UX research is.

This repo is not a gallery of polished visuals or a pixel-perfect carousel of screens. It is a folder. A folder full of my systems thinking, decision-making, reflection, problem-solving, and storytelling. I use it to share how I approach experience research and strategy.

Because here’s the thing. I’m not solely a visual designer. I never set out to be one. And while I understand the purpose of and deeply value design craft, I don’t believe compelling visuals should be the price of entry to share serious UX work. Especially when that work is about complex systems, inclusive content, user trust, and civic impact. UX also impacts many other aspects of life besides the work you do for your job.

The market is changing. What employers value is shifting. And while that settles, I wanted to build a portfolio that reflects how I think, how I learn, and how I solve. Not just what I shipped.

If visuals are included, it is because they serve the story, not the other way around.

So here it is. A living repository of my UX thinking. Some of it is polished. Some of it is rough. But all of it is mine. And all of it reflects the kind of practitioner I am. This space is not for approval. It is for articulation.

That’s the kind of UX work I want to do. Hopefully, it resonates.
