There’s something surprisingly revealing about looking around your home and realizing your physical clutter mirrors your mental clutter.

When I was laid off, the first wave of emotion was disorientation: 'What now?' But it quickly gave way to curiosity: 'What if I treated this moment like a design sprint? Not just for career recalibration, but for my life at home?'

So I did what any UX strategist might do. I audited my surroundings. My home office was a patchwork of beautiful heirlooms, overstuffed storage, snack drawers disguised as printer stations, and —my nemesis— paper piles. I couldn’t focus. I couldn’t prioritize. And that was my insight. My clutter wasn’t just a mess, it was _friction_.

Could I build a system that reduced friction in my real life the same way UX reduces digital friction?

I designed a household operating system that combines a physical binder setup with a digital dashboard (utilizing Google Drive, Docs, and Calendar). I even wrote a README for my folders. With the help of ChatGPT prompts and a bit of systems thinking, I created a modular structure that could scale, adapt, and, most importantly, be shared with my husband. No more asking “Where’s the car title?” or “When did we last service the HVAC?”

Now, that system supports everything from job applications to insurance claims to appliance maintenance.

The lesson? Sometimes UX isn’t a job. It’s a way of making life more livable. This project helped me feel more grounded, more equipped, and more confident, even in uncertain situations.
